GEO (generative engine optimization) and AEO (answer engine optimization) get
thrown around like they need an entirely new discipline. Mostly, they don't —
they change *what you're optimizing for*, not the fundamentals of good SEO.

## The shift: from ranking to being quoted

Traditional SEO optimizes to be the link someone clicks. GEO and AEO optimize
to be the source an AI system pulls from — and summarizes, rephrases, or
quotes — without the person necessarily clicking through at all.

That changes what "winning" looks like. You can rank well and still not get
cited in an AI Overview, and you can get cited without ranking #1.

## What actually helps

- **Direct, extractable answers.** If a question has a factual answer, state
  it plainly near the top — don't bury it under three paragraphs of
  preamble. Generative systems reward content that's easy to lift.
- **Clear entity structure.** Say who, what, and how it relates to other
  known entities explicitly. Structured data (schema.org markup) still
  matters here — it's one of the clearest signals you can give a machine.
- **Original information.** Rephrased conventional wisdom has nothing to
  offer a system that's already synthesized that conventional wisdom from a
  hundred other sources. A real data point, a specific case, an unusual
  result — those get pulled forward.
- **Genuine topical depth**, not keyword density. Generative systems are
  better at detecting thin, keyword-stuffed content than traditional search
  ever was.

## What doesn't change

Technical crawlability, site speed, and a coherent site structure still
matter — AI crawlers still have to reach and parse your content before any
of the above applies. GEO and AEO are additive to technical SEO, not a
replacement for it.

The honest version: this is still evolving, and best practices here will
keep shifting for a while. Treat anything (including this post) written
about GEO/AEO right now as directional, not gospel.
