Local SEO gets treated like a niche skill, but most of what breaks it is
depressingly basic. Before touching anything advanced, I check the same four
things every time.

## 1. Is the Google Business Profile actually claimed and complete?

Sounds obvious. It's still, by far, the most common gap I find — profiles
that are unclaimed, have the wrong category, or haven't been touched since
they were set up. This is the single highest-leverage fix available for a
local business, and it's free.

## 2. Is NAP consistent everywhere?

Name, Address, Phone — if these don't match exactly across your website,
Google Business Profile, and every directory you're listed on, you're making
Google work harder to trust that you're a real, single, stable business.
Inconsistency here is a quiet tax on every other local ranking factor.

## 3. Do you have location pages that say something real?

A location page that's just the homepage copy with a city name swapped in
doesn't help anyone. It needs the details that actually differ by location:
service area, local team, address, hours, maybe testimonials from that area.

## 4. Are reviews being actively managed?

Not just collected — responded to. Review volume and velocity matter, but so
does showing that someone's paying attention. This is usually the first
thing to slip once a business gets busy, and it shows.

## Where this actually pays off

None of this is complicated. It's just tedious enough that it gets skipped in
favor of more interesting-sounding work. In my experience, doing these four
things properly outperforms almost any "advanced" local SEO tactic you could
try instead.
